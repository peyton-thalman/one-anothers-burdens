## Chapter 9: We Have Renounced Dishonesty
*Manual themes: Building trust, Honest talk in the family, Honesty with ourselves, Honesty with God, Regular communication*

> "[We] have renounced the hidden things of dishonesty, not walking in craftiness, nor handling the word of God deceitfully; but by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God." (2 Corinthians 4:2)

### The Big Idea
Affliction invites hiding. Families keep a diagnosis quiet, avoid hard conversations, tell children "everything's fine," or pretend at church that nothing is wrong. Sometimes we hide things from ourselves, refusing to admit how bad it is or how tired we are. And sometimes we hide from God, praying polite prayers instead of honest ones. This principle invites truthfulness, offered with love, at every level. Trust grows where people can tell each other the truth.

### What the Scriptures Teach
- **2 Corinthians 4:2.** Paul describes giving up "the hidden things."
- **Ephesians 4:15, 25.** "Speaking the truth in love" and "speak every man truth with his neighbour."
- **Psalm 62:8.** "Pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us."
- **Psalm 13:1.** "How long wilt thou forget me, O Lord? for ever?" The Psalms are full of honest complaint, and God kept them in scripture.
- **Matthew 26:38.** In Gethsemane the Savior told His friends plainly, "My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death: tarry ye here, and watch with me."

### From Prophets and Apostles
> "Black clouds may also form in our lives, which can blind us to God's light and even cause us to question if that light exists for us anymore. Some of those clouds are of depression, anxiety, and other forms of mental and emotional affliction." — Sister Reyna I. Aburto, ["Thru Cloud and Sunshine, Lord, Abide with Me!"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2019/10/31aburto?lang=eng) (October 2019)

> "Does this mean we will always understand our challenges? Won't all of us, sometime, have reason to ask, 'O God, where art thou?' Yes!" — Elder Robert D. Hales, ["Waiting upon the Lord: Thy Will Be Done"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2011/10/waiting-upon-the-lord-thy-will-be-done?lang=eng) (October 2011)

> "Isn't it interesting that the one who is suffering often gains faith through suffering and accepts the Lord's will, 'thy will be done,' while family members and caregivers may have a difficult time accepting the tragic outcome and being able to strengthen their faith from the experience." — Elder Robert D. Hales, ["Finding Faith in the Lord Jesus Christ"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2004/10/finding-faith-in-the-lord-jesus-christ?lang=eng) (October 2004)

### In Real Life
- **Honesty with children.** Kids notice far more than adults think. Age-appropriate truth ("Grandma's brain is sick, and that's why she forgets things") is usually less frightening to them than silence.
- **Honesty with doctors and leaders.** Tell your loved one's care team what you actually see at home. Tell your bishop what you actually need.
- **Honesty with yourself.** Name how you're really doing. "I'm fine" said every day for a year usually isn't true.
- **Honesty with God.** He can handle your anger, your doubt, and your questions. Honest prayers are still prayers.
- **Regular check-ins.** In a family facing affliction, set a regular time to talk about how everyone is doing, not just the patient.

### Also Worth Knowing
- **Secrets are heavy.** Research on secrecy by psychologist Michael Slepian suggests that the burden of a secret comes less from hiding it in conversation and more from how often our minds return to it. Sharing it with even one trusted person can lighten that load.
- **Writing helps.** James Pennebaker's studies found that people who wrote honestly about their deepest feelings on a hard experience for 15 to 20 minutes over a few days often showed better mood and health afterward.

### Questions Worth Sitting With
1. What are you not saying out loud, to someone else, to yourself, or to God?
2. What do your children or other family members know about what's happening? What might they be imagining?
3. When did you last pray a completely honest prayer? What happened?
4. Who is one person you could tell the whole truth to this week?

### Practice Card: Week 9
**Main practice: The honest prayer**
Once this week, find a private place and pray aloud for at least ten minutes. Tell Heavenly Father exactly how you feel about your situation, including the parts you think you shouldn't feel. Then sit quietly for a few minutes and listen.
> *My plan:* On ______ (day), when ______ (the house is quiet / I'm on my walk), I will pray an honest prayer.

**Also try:**
- **Family check-in.** Ask each person at dinner, "How are you really doing with all this?" and just listen.
- **Write it out.** Spend 15 minutes writing everything you feel about this trial. You don't have to keep it.

- **Talks:** Reyna I. Aburto, ["Thru Cloud and Sunshine, Lord, Abide with Me!"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2019/10/31aburto?lang=eng) · Robert D. Hales, ["Finding Faith in the Lord Jesus Christ"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2004/10/finding-faith-in-the-lord-jesus-christ?lang=eng)
- **Scriptures:** Psalm 13 · Psalm 62 · Matthew 26:36–46 · Doctrine and Covenants 121:1–6
- **Books:** *Opening Up by Writing It Down*, James Pennebaker & Joshua Smyth

## Chapter 10: Lift Up the Hands Which Hang Down
*Manual themes: Supporting in righteousness, Encouragement, Continuing to minister, Serving others*

> "Wherefore, be faithful; stand in the office which I have appointed unto you; succor the weak, lift up the hands which hang down, and strengthen the feeble knees." (D&C 81:5)

### The Big Idea
This principle is about how to help the person who is suffering, and how serving others can heal us too. Good support is not the same as doing everything. It means encouraging, believing in the person, respecting their agency and dignity, and staying steady over time. It also means looking outward when you can. Many caregivers find that serving someone else, even in a small way, brings light back into their own life.

### What the Scriptures Teach
- **D&C 81:5.** "Succor," "lift," and "strengthen" are all words about helping someone stand, not carrying them forever.
- **Galatians 6:2.** "Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ."
- **Luke 10:33–34.** The Samaritan "had compassion on him, And went to him, and bound up his wounds." Then he arranged ongoing care at the inn.
- **Mosiah 2:17.** "When ye are in the service of your fellow beings ye are only in the service of your God."
- **John 13:14–15.** The Savior washed His disciples' feet and said, "I have given you an example."

### From Prophets and Apostles
> "In addition, we must notice the tribulation of others and try to help. That will be especially hard when we are being sorely tested ourselves. But we will discover as we lift another's burden, even a little, that our backs are strengthened and we sense a light in the darkness." — President Henry B. Eyring, ["Tested, Proved, and Polished"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2020/10/51eyring?lang=eng) (October 2020)

> "While God is at work making those repairs, the rest of us can help by being merciful, nonjudgmental, and kind." — Elder Jeffrey R. Holland, ["Like a Broken Vessel"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2013/10/like-a-broken-vessel?lang=eng) (October 2013)

> "The challenges and difficulties were not immediately removed from the people. But Alma and his followers were strengthened, and their increased capacity made the burdens lighter." — Elder David A. Bednar, ["Bear Up Their Burdens with Ease"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2014/04/bear-up-their-burdens-with-ease?lang=eng) (April 2014)

### In Real Life
- **Encourage without pushing.** "I believe in you" and "I'm proud of how hard you're working" go further than advice.
- **Respect their agency and dignity.** Let your loved one make the choices they're able to make, even small ones, like what to wear or which treatment to try. Being asked matters.
- **Stay steady.** Many people help in the first few weeks of a crisis and then fade away. Being the person who is still there in month eight means a lot.
- **Serve someone else.** Caregivers sometimes find relief in serving outside the situation: visiting someone else in the hospital, helping another family who's just been diagnosed, or simply doing a ministering visit.
- **If you're on the outside of someone else's affliction,** notice the caregiver too. Bring the meal to the spouse, not just the patient. Ask how they are doing.

### Also Worth Knowing
- **Helping helps the helper.** Studies on volunteering and helping behavior find links with lower stress and better mood for the person giving help. This fits what President Eyring describes: lifting someone else strengthens our own backs.
- **Autonomy supports recovery.** Across health psychology, people who feel they have some choice and control in their care tend to be more motivated and do better. Doing everything for someone can quietly take that away.

### Questions Worth Sitting With
1. Is there anything you're doing for your loved one that they could do, or would want to do, themselves?
2. What kind of encouragement does your loved one respond to best?
3. Who else do you know who is carrying a heavy load right now? What small thing could you do for them?
4. Who has lifted your hands during this trial? Have you told them what it meant?

### Practice Card: Week 10
**Main practice: Lift someone else**
Once this week, do one small act of service for someone outside your own situation: a note, a meal, a visit, a phone call.
> *My plan:* This week I will ______ for ______, on ______ (day).

**Also try:**
- **One sentence of encouragement.** Each day, tell your loved one one specific thing you admire about how they're handling this.
- **Ask, don't assume.** Ask your loved one, "What would actually help you most this week?"

### Go Deeper
- **Talks:** Henry B. Eyring, ["Tested, Proved, and Polished"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2020/10/51eyring?lang=eng) · Jean B. Bingham, ["Ministering as the Savior Does"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2018/04/ministering-as-the-savior-does?lang=eng) · Henry B. Eyring, ["Inspired Ministering"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2018/04/inspired-ministering?lang=eng)
- **Scriptures:** Luke 10:25–37 · Mosiah 18:8–10 · John 13:1–17
- **Books:** *Being Mortal*, Atul Gawande (on dignity and choice in serious illness)

## Chapter 11: Bear All These Things with Patience
*Manual themes: Healing is a process, Setbacks and hard days, When healing doesn't come here, Helping through setbacks*

> "For I know that thou wast in bonds; yea … thou didst bear all these things with patience because the Lord was with thee; and now thou knowest that the Lord did deliver thee." (Alma 38:4)

### The Big Idea
Most afflictions don't follow a straight line. Treatments work and then stop working. A loved one in recovery relapses. Grief that seemed to be easing comes back on an anniversary. Some afflictions will not be healed in this life at all. Patience, in the gospel sense, isn't passive. It is choosing to keep going, keep loving, and keep trusting God over a long road, including the setbacks. Alma's son Shiblon was delivered, but only after he had borne his bonds "with patience because the Lord was with" him.

### What the Scriptures Teach
- **Alma 38:4.** The Lord was with Shiblon during the bonds, not only at the deliverance.
- **D&C 24:8.** "Be patient in afflictions, for thou shalt have many; but endure them, for, lo, I am with thee, even unto the end of thy days."
- **2 Corinthians 12:7–9.** Paul prayed three times for his "thorn in the flesh" to be removed. The Lord's answer was, "My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness."
- **D&C 121:7–8.** "Thine adversity and thine afflictions shall be but a small moment; And then, if thou endure it well, God shall exalt thee on high."
- **Lamentations 3:22–23.** "His compassions fail not. They are new every morning." Each hard day can start over.

### From Prophets and Apostles
> "There is an important concept here: patience is not passive resignation, nor is it failing to act because of our fears. Patience means active waiting and enduring. It means staying with something and doing all that we can—working, hoping, and exercising faith; bearing hardship with fortitude, even when the desires of our hearts are delayed. Patience is not simply enduring; it is enduring well!" — President Dieter F. Uchtdorf, ["Continue in Patience"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2010/04/continue-in-patience?lang=eng) (April 2010)

> "Recognize that some challenges in life will not be resolved here on earth. Paul pled thrice that 'a thorn in the flesh' be removed. The Lord simply answered, 'My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.'" — Elder Richard G. Scott, ["To Be Healed"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/1994/04/to-be-healed?lang=eng) (April 1994)

> "For every infirm man healed instantly as he waits to enter the Pool of Bethesda, someone else will spend 40 years in the desert waiting to enter the promised land." — Elder Jeffrey R. Holland, ["Waiting on the Lord"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2020/10/57holland?lang=eng) (October 2020)

> "Some blessings come soon, some come late, and some don't come until heaven; but for those who embrace the gospel of Jesus Christ, they come." — Elder Jeffrey R. Holland, ["An High Priest of Good Things to Come"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/1999/10/an-high-priest-of-good-things-to-come?lang=eng) (October 1999)

### In Real Life
- **Relapse in addiction or mental illness** is common and doesn't erase progress. Respond with calm, clear support and the limits you've already set, not panic or shame.
- **Flare-ups in chronic illness** can feel like going back to the beginning. They usually aren't. Track the longer trend, not just this week.
- **Grief comes in waves.** Birthdays, holidays, and anniversaries often bring it back. Plan for those days and let people know.
- **When the answer is "not in this life."** Some families have to accept that a disability will stay, a disease is terminal, or a loved one may never return. That acceptance isn't giving up on God. Paul kept serving with his thorn. You can keep living, loving, and hoping with yours.

### Also Worth Knowing
- **Recovery isn't linear.** In addiction treatment, relapse is widely understood as a common part of recovery rather than proof of failure. Similar patterns show up in depression and many chronic illnesses.
- **Grief oscillates.** Margaret Stroebe and Henk Schut's "dual process model" describes healthy grieving as moving back and forth between facing the loss and taking breaks from it to handle daily life. Having good days doesn't mean you didn't love the person.

### Questions Worth Sitting With
1. What setback has been hardest for you so far? How did you respond, and what would you do the same or differently?
2. Where is the "longer trend" better than it feels this week?
3. Is there an affliction in your family you may need to accept won't end in this life? What would that acceptance look like with faith?
4. What does "enduring well" mean to you, in practical terms, this month?

### Practice Card: Week 11
**Main practice: A setback plan**
Write down what you'll do the next time there's a bad day, a relapse, or a flare-up: who you'll call, what you'll say to yourself, which scripture you'll read, and one kind thing you'll do for yourself.
> *My plan:* When the next hard day comes, I will ______, call ______, and read ______.

**Also try:**
- **Mark the hard dates.** Put upcoming anniversaries or appointments on the calendar with a reminder to plan support for that day.
- **Look back.** Write down three ways things are better than they were six months ago, even small ones.

## Chapter 12: My Peace I Give unto You
*Manual themes: Finding peace, Forgiveness, God's help to forgive, Hope beyond this life*

> "Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid." (John 14:27)

### The Big Idea
The Savior offers a peace that doesn't depend on circumstances. He said it is "not as the world giveth." The world's peace comes when problems are solved. His comes even while they aren't. Part of receiving it is letting go of what poisons the heart: resentment toward a loved one whose choices hurt the family, anger at a doctor or a relative, bitterness at how unfair it all is. Forgiveness doesn't excuse wrong or require you to stay in harm's way. It frees you. And the deepest peace comes from knowing how the story ends: every wrong made right, every loss restored, and every tear wiped away.

### What the Scriptures Teach
- **John 14:27.** He spoke these words on the night before His death, knowing what was coming.
- **Philippians 4:7.** "The peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus."
- **D&C 64:10.** "I, the Lord, will forgive whom I will forgive, but of you it is required to forgive all men."
- **John 16:33.** "In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world."
- **Revelation 21:4.** "God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain."

### From Prophets and Apostles
> "Through His infinite Atonement, you can forgive those who have hurt you and who may never accept responsibility for their cruelty to you." — President Russell M. Nelson, as quoted by Sister Kristin M. Yee, ["Beauty for Ashes: The Healing Path of Forgiveness"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2022/10/23yee?lang=eng) (October 2022)

> "On the path of forgiveness, Jesus Christ's atoning power can flow into our lives and begin to heal the deep crevasses of the heart and soul." — Sister Kristin M. Yee, ["Beauty for Ashes: The Healing Path of Forgiveness"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2022/10/23yee?lang=eng) (October 2022)

> "The truth is, you will never completely get over it until you are together once again with your departed loved ones. I will never have a fulness of joy until we are reunited in the morning of the First Resurrection." — Elder Shayne M. Bowen, ["'Because I Live, Ye Shall Live Also'"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2012/10/because-i-live-ye-shall-live-also?lang=eng) (October 2012)

> "Know that one day the dawn will break brightly and all shadows of mortality will flee." — Elder Jeffrey R. Holland, ["Like a Broken Vessel"](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2013/10/like-a-broken-vessel?lang=eng) (October 2013)

### In Real Life
- **Forgiving a loved one.** If the affliction involves someone's choices, you may carry real hurt. Forgiveness can begin before trust is rebuilt, and it doesn't erase the limits you've set.
- **Forgiving others.** A careless comment at church, a relative who disappeared when you needed them, a medical mistake. Holding onto these keeps the wound open.
- **Forgiving yourself.** For the times you lost patience, missed something, or said the wrong thing. The Savior's Atonement covers your mistakes as a caregiver too.
- **Anger at God.** Many people feel it, and it's better to bring it to Him than to hide it. Peace often comes after that kind of honest wrestling.
- **Peace in grief.** The plan of salvation doesn't remove the ache of losing someone, but it changes what the ache means. Separation is temporary.

### Also Worth Knowing
- **Forgiveness and health.** Studies by Everett Worthington, Robert Enright, and others link forgiveness with lower stress, less anxiety and depression, and better physical health. Researchers are careful to separate forgiveness from reconciliation: you can forgive someone without restoring a relationship that isn't safe.
- **"Continuing bonds."** Grief researchers (Klass, Silverman, and Nickman, 1996) found that many bereaved people keep a meaningful connection with the person who died, and that this is healthy, not a failure to move on. Latter-day Saint belief in eternal families gives that connection deep roots.

### Questions Worth Sitting With
1. Who do you need to forgive in this trial, including possibly yourself?
2. What would change in your heart if you let go of one specific resentment this week?
3. When have you felt the Savior's peace even though nothing around you had changed?
4. How does your belief in the Resurrection and eternal families shape how you face this affliction?

### Practice Card: Week 12
**Main practice: Begin forgiving**
Choose one person you need to forgive. Each day this week, pray for them by name and ask the Savior to help you let go of the hurt. You don't need to feel it yet. Start with the prayer.
> *My plan:* When I ______ (kneel for evening prayer / finish scripture study), I will pray for ______ by name.

**Also try:**
- **Look ahead.** Read Alma 40:23 and Revelation 21:1–4 and write down what you most look forward to being made right.
- **Look back.** Reread your comfort list from Week 1 and your "God's hand" notes from Week 7. Notice how much you've been carried.

## Appendix A: Finding support

### People and places to turn to
- **Your bishop or branch president** can provide counsel, help with practical needs, and refer you to Family Services for counseling.
- **Relief Society and elders quorum presidencies and your ministering brothers and sisters** can organize meals, rides, childcare, and visits.
- **Family Services** offers counseling and other help through referral from your bishop.
- **The Church's Addiction Recovery Program** runs support groups for family members as well as for people in recovery: [addictionrecovery.churchofjesuschrist.org](https://addictionrecovery.churchofjesuschrist.org)
- **Life Help** on the Church's website has resources on topics like mental health, disability, grief, abuse, and suicide: [churchofjesuschrist.org/study/life-help](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/life-help?lang=eng)
- **Community and medical resources:** hospital social workers, hospice and palliative care teams, disease-specific organizations, NAMI (mental illness), and local grief and caregiver groups.

### Choosing a support person
The original guide encourages each person to have at least one support person: someone you can call when things are hard. A good support person:

- Keeps what you tell them confidential
- Listens more than they advise
- Points you toward the Savior without preaching at you
- Is steady and available, and honest with you when you need it

It can be a spouse, a friend, a family member, a ministering brother or sister, or someone who has been through a similar trial.

### Being a good support person for someone else
- **Show up and keep showing up.** Consistency matters more than eloquence.
- **Ask, then listen.** "How are you doing with all of this?" followed by silence is often the most helpful thing you can offer.
- **Offer specific help.** "I'm bringing dinner Thursday" is easier to accept than "Let me know if you need anything."
- **Don't explain their trial to them.** Avoid "Everything happens for a reason" and "At least…". Mourn with them first.
- **Remember the caregiver.** The person caring for the patient is often running on empty.

## Appendix B: For group leaders

### A simple meeting outline (about 50 minutes)
1. **Welcome and prayer** (3 min)
2. **Report** (10 min): How did last week's Practice Card go? Celebrate small wins. Nobody has to share.
3. **Read the Big Idea and the scriptures together** (10 min)
4. **Discussion** (20 min): Pick two or three of the Questions Worth Sitting With. Use the quotes if things stall.
5. **This week's Practice Card** (5 min): Each person fills in their own plan.
6. **Closing prayer** (2 min)

### Keeping the group safe
- **What's said in the group stays in the group.**
- **Share your own experience,** not advice for others, unless someone asks.
- **No comparing trials.** Everyone's affliction is hard for them.
- **Watch for warning signs.** If anyone mentions thoughts of suicide, abuse, or danger, follow up privately, share the crisis numbers, and involve the bishop.
